MathHell: Balance Home Support

Privacy Policy

Last updated: July 11, 2026

● The short version

MathHell: Balance needs an online leaderboard to be fun, so it is not a zero-data app — but it collects the minimum a game like this needs, and nothing is ever sold.

  • You can play entirely as a guest — no email, no real name required. A random player name (like “Euler-42”) is generated for you.
  • The leaderboard stores your display name and best scores — nothing else identifies you publicly.
  • Sign-in with Google or Apple is optional and only exists so your record survives reinstalls and device changes.
  • We use standard crash reporting and analytics to keep the game working, and rewarded ads are shown only when you choose to watch one (currently non-personalized).
  • You can delete your account and all your leaderboard records from inside the app at any time.

# Data We Collect

Account. On first launch the game creates an anonymous account (a random technical identifier from Firebase Authentication). If you later sign in with Google or Apple, your account identifier and the display name provided by that service are linked to it. We never see your password, and we do not request your contacts, photos, or location.

Leaderboard. When a run ends, the game submits your display name, score, level and question reached, answer statistics (correct / wrong / timeouts), run duration, a technical run identifier, and whether an ad-revive was used. Your display name and score are visible to other players on the public leaderboard.

Settings. Sound, vibration, sensitivity, language, and similar preferences are stored only on your device.

# Analytics and Crash Reporting

To find bugs and understand what to improve, the app uses:

  • Google Firebase (Analytics, Crashlytics, Remote Config) — anonymized usage events, crash logs, device model and OS version, and app-instance identifiers. Firebase privacy.
  • Sentry — crash and error reports (stack traces, device model, OS version). Events are processed in the EU. Sentry privacy.

These reports do not include your math answers or any content you type; the game has no free-text input besides your player name.

# Advertising

The game may offer an optional rewarded ad — for example, to continue a run after losing. Ads appear only when you tap the offer; there are no banners or forced interstitials. Ads are served by Google AdMob and are currently requested in non-personalized mode, meaning they are based on context rather than a profile of you. The ad SDK may still process device information (such as identifiers, IP address, and coarse diagnostics) for fraud prevention and frequency capping under Google's privacy policy. If personalized ads are ever introduced, the app will ask for the required consent first (App Tracking Transparency on iOS and GDPR consent where applicable).

# Where Your Data Lives

Leaderboard and account data are stored in Google Firebase (Firestore). Crash reports go to Sentry (EU ingestion) and Firebase Crashlytics. These providers act as processors on our behalf and may store data on servers in the EU and the United States. We do not sell your data, and we do not share it with anyone beyond these service providers.

# Your Choices and Deletion

  • Play as a guest — never sign in; your leaderboard entry is tied only to a random identifier and generated name.
  • Change your player name at any time in Settings.
  • Delete your account in Settings → Delete account. This permanently removes your leaderboard records, your best-results data, and the account itself. It cannot be undone.
  • You can also email us (below) to request access to or deletion of your data.

# Device Permissions

The game uses device motion (accelerometer and gyroscope) to control the beam — motion data is processed on the device in real time and never recorded or transmitted. It does not use location, camera, microphone, or contacts.

# Children's Privacy

The app is not directed at children under 13, does not knowingly collect personal information from them, and serves only non-personalized ads. Signing in with Google or Apple requires an account with those services, which have their own age requirements. If you believe a child has provided personal information through the app, contact us and we will delete it.
